1 · The palette

Fourteen values, each a role. The whole interface is allowed to use nothing else. The rule the shipping palette is built on: accent is pink because yellow is the terminal's own chrome colour, and an accent that appears everywhere is not an accent.

<% .Swatches %>

The light (“marble & wine”) preset this page used to offer is gone on purpose: no palette here paints the terminal's own background, so a light-ground preset could be shown in a browser and never chosen in the terminal. theme.go carries the reasoning, and what it does instead — three text roles corrected for a light terminal — is not a palette and is not offered as one.

4 · The glyph family

Every non-alphabetic character the interface may draw is built from one small vocabulary: the half blocks — the top pixel <% .G.H %>, which every rule is made of; the bottom pixel <% .G.Think %>, which the thought figure and the accent are made of; both, <% .G.Tool %> — the two halves of a cell <% .G.Seg %><% .G.Nub %>, the quadrant mark <% .G.Tick %>, and the box-drawing rules <% .G.Hair %><% .G.V %> <% .G.Branch %><% .G.Last %>. A terminal cell is two pixels tall, so a half block is the smallest mark there is — which is why the chrome and the creatures come out of the same material and the screen reads as one thing.

Weight is pixel coverage: a half-block rule is the loud one, a hairline the quiet one. Nothing needs a patched icon font, and one set covers every case, so there is no wide variant and no narrow one.

6 · The creatures

Three figures, five marks by five rows each, drawn at two cells to a mark: a ten-cell-wide, ten-pixel-tall sprite. █ body · ▓▒ detail · ▒ highlight · ▄ the one accent pixel · eyes are holes, never drawn shapes.

<% .Gallery %>

Why three small ones and not the cat

Nyx was 20 rows × 28 marks (56 cells) with four tones, a ringed tail, mascara lines, a spotted belly and a walk cycle in four frames — three of which differ only in the legs. Every change meant redrawing four frames and re-pinning a copy in the installer. That is a lot of art for a mascot whose whole job is to say "hello, I am not a hang" on an empty screen.

A creature here is 25 marks per pose and three poses, and the fourth frame — the blink — is derived rather than drawn: it is the idle frame with the eye holes filled, so it cannot fall out of step with the body it blinks on.

The rules the next creature must keep

rulewhy
Eyes are negative spacedrawn eyes plus a mouth read as a glare with teeth at this size — shipped once, rejected
Exactly one accent run per framemore than one and the brand colour stops being a detail and becomes decoration; a test enforces it
Markers, never coloursshape and palette stay one thing each: one table turns a marker into a glyph, a colour and a role
Plain monospace onlyno Nerd Font requirement — every mark is a half block or a dither from the ramp
Tone from the 14-value palettea creature with private colours cannot be re-themed with the rest of the interface
5 marks × 5 rows, every poseredrawable in half a minute, and dimensionally identical frames so animation cannot move the layout

The four poses each creature wears: idle (the one the welcome screen shows), blink (derived), bob (the other foot) and thinking (attending). Marker source and the full reasoning: docs/mascots.md, generated from the same values.

What must not change, whatever the theme

Fourteen colours and no more · eyes as negative space · one accent run per creature · no Nerd Fonts · the accent never takes a colour the terminal already uses for chrome (yellow is warning) · every colour is a role, never a hue, so a second palette stays a value and not a rewrite · every glyph's width declared and measured, never counted by eye.
